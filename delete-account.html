Delete your account and data

This page exists so the request can be made from outside the app — without installing anything and without asking a moderator first. It describes exactly what deletion removes, what it cannot reach, and why.

Who actually holds your data

Asbern is self-hosted. The bot runs on a computer belonging to whoever installed it — normally the owner of your Discord server — and your data is stored on that machine. We do not operate a service that receives it. So for a self-hosted server the deletion is carried out by that server’s owner, not by us, and the routes below are how you reach them.

If you signed in to the Asbern mobile app against our own service, we hold that sign-in record and we act on the request ourselves.

Three ways to ask

  • In the mobile app — Settings → Account → Delete account. You are asked to type a confirmation phrase, because this cannot be undone.
  • In Discord — /menu → Profile can clear your history, and where the AI companions are enabled it can show you everything they hold about you and delete all of it.
  • By asking the server owner — on a self-hosted install they are the data controller, and the admin console has a per-member erase.

What deletion removes

  • Your sign-in record and any stored authentication tokens, both on the device and on the server.
  • Your progression — level, XP, achievements, and your row in every leaderboard.
  • Your currency balance, inventory and transaction ledger.
  • Your watch history, resume points and favourites.
  • Your activity counters and voice co-presence records.
  • Everything the AI companions hold about you, transcripts and summaries alike, where that feature is enabled.
  • Support tickets you opened, and the answers you typed into their forms.

What deletion cannot reach, and why

This list is here because it would be easy to leave out. A deletion page that promises more than the software does is worse than one that is specific.
Data that survives account deletion, and the reason
WhatWhy it survives
Your messages in Discord They belong to Discord and to the server, not to Asbern. Delete them in Discord, or ask a moderator. Asbern does not store message content outside the narrow, disclosed cases in the privacy policy.
Moderation records about you Warnings, bans and case notes are the server’s record of its own decisions. A member erasing the account of why they were actioned would defeat the point of keeping it. Ask the server owner.
Server-wide totals you contributed to A counting channel’s current number, or a server’s all-time message count, is one shared figure rather than a pile of per-member rows. Your per-member share is removed; the aggregate is not recomputed downwards.
Backups, for up to 7 days Daily snapshots roll on a seven-day window. Your data leaves the live store immediately and ages out of the backups within a week. Restoring a backup inside that window would bring it back — that is the trade a backup is.
Operational log entries Raw entries age out after 90 days. They record that an action happened, not what was said.
Transcripts an administrator exported Once a file has been downloaded it is outside the software entirely. Ask whoever exported it.

How long it takes

The live deletion is immediate — the record is gone when the request returns, not queued for a nightly job. Backups age out within 7 days and raw operational log entries within 90 days, as above.

If you come back afterwards

You start over. Deletion is not a reset with a copy kept behind it: there is no retained version to restore, which is the entire point. If you only want to step away, simply leaving the server keeps your progression — the retention table covers that case.

If none of those work

A contact address is not published yet. Printing “email us” beside nothing would be worse than saying so plainly. Until there is one, the in-app and in-Discord routes above are the working ones — and on a self-hosted server the owner is the data controller and can always act.
