Getting started

Adding Asbern, in order.

Three steps, and only one of them happens on this website. The server you are adding it to is chosen on Discord’s own authorisation screen, not here — we do not ask you to sign in first and we do not keep a list of your servers.

The public install is not open yet. Asbern runs in production in one community, and the Discord application it would be installed from is not published — the verification that lets a bot pass 100 servers has not been applied for, and its two required legal URLs are still drafts. Everything on this page is real and accurate; the last button is the one thing that does not work, and it says so rather than sending you to a broken authorisation screen.
1
Pick a permission set
On this page, below

Three presets, and you can change your mind later by re-inviting. Every permission Asbern asks for is listed with the sentence describing what stops working without it — not the bit name, the consequence.

2
Authorise on Discord
discord.com, not here

Discord shows you the permission list and its own server picker. It offers the servers where you hold Manage Server. If a server is not in that list, that is Discord telling you your own permissions, and nothing we can change.

3
Run /menu once
In the server

On a server with nothing configured, an administrator running /menu gets the first-run walkthrough instead of the dashboard: the required settings, in order, with what each one unlocks. Members see the normal menu. Nothing is left in a file for you to edit.

/menu is the only slash command. Watching, live TV, game servers, the casino, levels, profile, stats, help and the whole 221-control admin console all run from inside it, so there is no list of commands to learn and nothing else appears in your members’ command picker.
Step 1

Choose how much you are handing over.

Most bots ask for Administrator and explain nothing. Asbern degrades: a permission it does not have turns off the controls that need it and says which ones, rather than failing silently in the background.

Every permission, and what breaks without it

Discord permissions Asbern requests and the consequence of withholding each
Permission Min Rec Full Without it
Position matters as much as permission, and nobody tells you this. Permissions decide what Asbern may do; its role position decides who it may do it to. A bot with every permission and a low role can still do nothing — rank roles are never granted, silently, per member, forever. The dashboard flags every role sitting above Asbern at the picker, before you run anything.

Scopes are bot and applications.commands, and nothing else. Asbern does not use an OAuth code grant, does not read your Discord account, and never asks for your password.

Or run the whole thing yourself.

Asbern is a self-hosted bot first. You create your own Discord application, run it on your own machine, and no part of it reports to us — which is also why the privacy policy can say what it says. The one thing self-hosting cannot give you is the streaming relay, because a home upload line genuinely cannot serve twenty viewers.
