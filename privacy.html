Who we are

Asbern (“the Bot”) is a Discord application published by legal or trading name (“we”, “us”). You can contact us at contact email on a domain we control.

The most important thing to understand

The Bot is self-hosted. It runs on the computer or server belonging to the person who installed it — normally the owner of your Discord server. Its data is stored on that machine. We do not operate a service that receives it, and in the self-hosted configuration we cannot see it.

This means the server owner who runs the Bot is the party who controls your data. If you want to know what is held about you, or want it removed, they are who to ask — and the Bot gives them tools to answer you (see Your choices).

To be rewritten before a hosted version launches. The platform programme intends to run parts of Asbern on our own infrastructure — the relay, the dashboard and the API. On the day any of that carries member data, this section must say plainly which components run on our infrastructure, what reaches them, and where it is stored. Publishing a “we never see it” policy and then launching a service that does is the failure mode this note exists to prevent.

What the Bot collects

The Bot identifies you by your Discord user ID — a number. It does not collect your email address, phone number, real name, IP address, payment details or connected accounts, and it never sees your Discord password.

Depending on which features the server owner has enabled, the Bot may store:

  • Progression — your level, XP, prestige tier and achievements.
  • In-server currency — your balance, virtual assets and lifetime earnings. This currency has no real-world value.
  • Activity counters — how many messages you sent, in which channels, how many voice minutes you spent, how many reactions you gave and received, how many messages you edited or deleted, and whether a message contained a link or a file. These are counts only. The Bot does not store what you said, which links you posted, or the names of files you uploaded.
  • Who you spent time with in voice — for each minute you are in a voice channel with other people, a counter for each pair. This exists so the server’s statistics can describe the community, not just individuals.
  • Watch history — which titles and channels you watched through the screening room, how long for, and where you got to, so the Bot can offer to resume.
  • Counting game — how many valid counts you have made, and whether you were the last person to count.
  • Game servers — how many times you have started each game server.
  • Moderation records — warnings issued to you, if the server’s moderators use that feature.
  • Nicknames and roles — the Bot grants and removes roles as you level up or are given access, and records that a role changed, so the server owner can reconcile what the Bot did with what actually happened.

Message content

The Bot requests Discord’s Message Content privileged intent. It uses message text in only these ways:

  1. The counting game
    In the single channel the server owner nominates as the counting channel, the Bot reads each message to find a number at the start of it. It stores the number and nothing else. A message that does not begin with a digit is ignored completely. In every other channel, the counting code stops before looking at anything.
  2. A “links posted” counter
    For each message, the Bot checks whether the text contains a web address, and if so increases a counter. The address itself and the message text are not stored.
  3. Moderator clean-up tools
    A server administrator can ask the Bot to remove messages in one channel that contain a link, or that match text or a pattern they type. The Bot reads the messages to find matches, removes them, and keeps nothing.
  4. Channel archives
    When an administrator retires a whole category of channels, they can export a transcript of those channels so the history is not simply lost. That export does contain message text, along with author names and timestamps. It is written to a file on the machine the Bot runs on, under the administrator’s control. It is never sent to us.

Beyond those, the Bot does not log message content, does not archive chat, does not read direct messages, and does not use message content for advertising, profiling, resale or model training.

Diagnostic logs

The Bot writes a structured operational log — which actions ran, whether they succeeded, which rule refused a request, and how money moved. Its written rule is that message content and secrets are never logged, and the single deliberate exception is the counting game’s parsed number, because that is game state. Raw log files are automatically deleted after 90 days; summarised daily statistics, which contain no content, are kept.

The optional AI companion

The AI companion is built but ships switched off, and it is not sold today. This section describes what it does when a server owner enables it.

It is switched off by default and, when on, is limited to an allow-list of members the server owner chooses. When it is enabled and you are in a voice channel with it:

  • Your speech is transcribed and the text is sent to the configured AI provider so the companion can reply. name the provider(s) and link their policy
  • Transcripts are stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM) on the machine the Bot runs on, and raw transcripts are automatically deleted after 7 days. Condensed summaries are kept for about six months and then dropped.
  • You can mute yourself to the companion at any time from the Bot’s menu. While muted, your audio is not captured, transcribed or stored at all.
  • You can opt out of profiling, which erases what the companion has learned about you and stops it sharing anything about you.
  • You can delete everything the companion holds about you, at any time.
  • If you leave the server, the companion’s data about you is deleted automatically.

The AI companion does not read text chat. It runs as a separate Discord application which does not request the Message Content intent and cannot receive message text.

Who your data is shared with

Nobody, by default. The Bot does not sell data, does not share it with advertisers, and does not send it to us. Two exceptions, both chosen by the server owner:

  • If the optional AI companion is enabled, transcribed speech is sent to the configured AI provider in order to generate a reply.
  • If the server owner has configured an optional third-party statistics integration, the Bot may query that provider’s API for server-activity figures.

Your choices

From the Bot’s /menu → Profile you can:

  • Clear your history — remove your watch history and your game-server history.
  • If the AI is enabled: mute yourself to it, opt out of profiling, see what it holds about you, and delete all of it.

Server administrators can additionally:

  • Export everything the Bot holds about one member as a single document.
  • Delete a member’s data, within the limits below.

What we will not pretend

Some stores have no per-member deletion path, and the Bot says so rather than claiming a deletion it did not perform:

  • Currency balances cannot be individually erased, because the currency store has strict correctness guarantees and inventing a deletion path for it would bypass every safety check that protects everyone else’s balances.
  • Levels and XP have no per-member eraser, for the same reason.
  • Activity counters are stored as monthly files of aggregate cells with no per-member deletion path; rewriting them to remove one member risks destroying the whole month.

If you need those removed, contact the server owner. Because the Bot is self-hosted, they hold the files and can remove them directly — and because it is self-hosted, we cannot do it for you.

Children

The Bot is not directed at children. You must meet Discord’s minimum age requirement for your country to use Discord at all, and therefore to use the Bot.
