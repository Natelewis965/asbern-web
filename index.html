The part nobody else has

A watch party that plays your library, properly.

Every other Discord bot on the market can play music. None of them can put the film on. Asbern reads the Plex server you already run, transcodes it beside the files, and hands out one public link. What arrives is a real 1080p stream, not a re-encode of a re-encode of somebody's window.

One link. No accounts.

Tap Watch and you get a single-use link of your own. No Plex login, no invite, no "can someone add me to the server". People who are not in your voice channel — people who are on a phone in a car park — can still be in the room.

Films, shows and live TV.

Around 150 curated live channels sit alongside the library, joined to a guide so the picker shows what is actually on. A show auto-advances episode to episode while anyone is still watching. A dead channel is caught, announced and flagged to moderators.

It knows who actually turned up.

Authorisation runs on every segment of the stream, so presence is measured at the player — not guessed from voice presence. That is what pays out watch time, resumes you where you stopped, builds the end-of-film roster, and shuts the screen off after fifteen empty minutes.

Ten modules

Most of the market’s paid features are our free tier.

Levelling, moderation, profiles, stats and 163 of the 221 admin controls cost nothing, permanently — that is not a trial with a clock on it. What you pay for is the economy and the history, and then the things that need real hardware and real bandwidth. Each module is gated in the runner rather than on the button, so a module you have not paid for cannot half-work.
